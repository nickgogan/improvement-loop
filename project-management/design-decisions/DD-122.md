## Why

**Second consecutive proceed with a strengthening signal.** The trigger fired at 37 findings (2026-07-16) and again at 42 (2026-07-19); the 5 wave-4 findings split 4:1 toward the unattended/autonomous half, widening the gap rather than blurring it. Both destinations clear the DD-102 sub-guide viability floor (≥12 clean findings): G3c has 22 clean, G3d has 15 clean.

**Two distinct reader intents.** "Harden a supervised production workflow" (plan/execute separation, state, observability, cost, degradation, review bandwidth, quality-at-source, routing) is a different question from "remove the human from the loop" (scheduling surfaces, trigger shapes, headless composition, loop contracts, autonomy levels, reconciliation queues). Carrying both in one guide forces a reader to navigate past the half they are not in.

**Shared substrate is duplicated deliberately, not contested.** The 5 shared findings (durable execution ×2, cost caps, degradation modes, YAML-DAG substrate) are the failure/execution contract common to both regimes; each destination needs them in place, so they appear in both `source_findings[]`. This is the normal ~12% shared tax, not a routing failure (0 contested).

## Rules

1. New Orchestration operate-stage findings route to G3c or G3d per the trigger-keyword partition, never to the deprecated source (DD-98 acceptance criterion: deprecated guides do not accept new findings).
2. The source guide body is retained intact in `archive/guides/`; it is read-only history, not a live substrate surface. The Librarian composes G3c/G3d, not the archived source.
3. Shared findings' `consumed_by` lists BOTH destination filenames. A-only and B-only findings list exactly one.
4. If a future regen of either destination crosses the DD-102 trigger again, the split procedure recurses per DD-98 — G3c/G3d are ordinary guides subject to the same lifecycle.
